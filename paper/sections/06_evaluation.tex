\section{Preregistered evaluation}

The primary event-native tasks are SHD with an SRNN and DVS Gesture with a
convolutional/recurrent SNN. N-MNIST with a feedforward SNN is a negative control
expected to be more transport-robust. SSC is deferred until all primary gates
pass. Five training seeds share frozen sample IDs and semantics variants.

The canonical training set is hash-partitioned into training, label-free repair
calibration, and unlabeled certificate audit splits. The canonical labeled test
set is sequestered. We vary reset, integration, threshold timing, precision,
rounding/saturation, and one-step delay separately, followed by four preregistered
high-risk combinations.

For every condition we will report actual absolute accuracy change, certificate
upper bound and slack, static certified-input fraction, sample complexity,
emulator--hardware disagreement, decisions at one-, two-, and five-point budgets,
repair recovery, remaining risk, label count, runtime, and hardware runs. Single-
axis experiments support causal attribution and provide counterexamples to
invalid equivalence claims.

The submission gate requires two physical backends, four conditions with more
than five points of uncorrected loss, no observed simultaneous-bound violation,
median slack at most three points, 90th-percentile slack at most eight points,
and at least 70\% label-free recovery across both tasks and backends. The method
must beat global threshold scaling and PTQ at matched cost. If more than 80\% of
inputs lack a nonvacuous static family certificate, or conformance dominates, we
pivot to a systems venue.

\subsection{Software evidence and an early formulation failure}

We completed the frozen software matrix on SHD for five independently trained
128-neuron SRNNs. The reference test accuracy is $71.5\pm3.0\%$ (mean and sample
standard deviation); this is sufficient for a semantic-sensitivity study but not
the reference-model quality we require for the final submission. Five of ten
target conditions lose more than five points on average. Reset-to-value loses
9.9 points, reset plus one-step delay loses 16.1 points, and floor-rounded fixed
point loses 53.6 points. Thus the recurrent transport failure is not a
single-seed artifact.

Across all 50 seed--condition cells, unlabeled audit disagreement strongly ranks
absolute test accuracy change (Pearson $r=0.989$, Spearman $\rho=0.957$). The
Bonferroni-corrected 95\% Clopper--Pearson limits have no observed violation.
They are nevertheless too loose for deployment: median slack is 17.6 points and
90th-percentile slack is 30.1 points, failing the preregistered three- and
eight-point gates by a large margin. Figure~\ref{fig:shd-software-pilot} shows
that the failure is not primarily finite-sample confidence inflation; empirical
disagreement itself substantially exceeds net accuracy change.

  \caption{Five-seed SHD software pilot (50 seed--condition cells). Every point
  lies above the tight-bound line, so coverage holds, but the distribution-free

The sound per-input analysis exposes an important distinction between discrete
and continuous families. An initial implementation merged output intervals from
mutually exclusive discrete semantics before testing the argmax. That operation
is sound but unnecessarily compares, for example, the reference-class logit from
one reset rule with a competitor from another. It certified only 18.79\% of SHD
inputs. We preserve that result but supersede it with member-wise checking:
continuous uncertainty is merged within each discrete member, the argmax is
proved there, and the family certificate is the conjunction of member results.

Across all 861 audit inputs and five seeds, this correction certifies 76.19\% for
reset, 84.81\% for integration, 83.00\% for timing or delay, and 94.96\% for
nearest fixed point. The finite 16-member high-risk family certifies 38.72\%,
ranging from 34.26\% to 42.39\% by seed. For unquantized point semantics it
matches exact family agreement, as it should. No static certificate contradicts
exact enumeration. The finite-family static gate therefore passes; the earlier
failure was an avoidable cross-member relaxation artifact.

The bounded-family result is different. We jointly vary timestep and neuron
threshold by only $\pm1\%$, $\pm2\%$, or $\pm5\%$ inside every one of the 16
discrete members. The sound interval domain certifies zero SHD inputs at every
radius for every seed. On fixed 64-input validation subsets, all tested box
corners preserve the reference prediction on 36.9\%, 32.5\%, and 24.7\% of
inputs, respectively, with no counterexample to a static certificate. Corner
agreement is diagnostic rather than a proof over the interior, but its separation
from zero sound coverage quantifies the abstraction failure. Figure~\ref{fig:shd-continuous}
shows that the declared stop rule still triggers for the manuscript's promised
continuous bounded-family claim, not for finite enumeration.

The five frozen convolutional/recurrent DVS Gesture models reach
$84.85\pm1.76\%$ reference test accuracy. Floor-rounded fixed point loses 36.6
points on average, whereas exponential integration loses 3.7 points and
reset-to-value loses 1.5 points; only the two floor conditions exceed five
points on average. Disagreement again ranks absolute test change (Pearson
$r=0.962$), and none of 50 simultaneous limits is violated. The certificate is
still operationally loose: median slack is 18.5 points and 90th-percentile slack
is 29.4 points. With only 104 audit recordings per seed, simultaneous confidence
also imposes a roughly five-point floor near zero observed disagreement.
Figure~\ref{fig:dvs-software} shows both regimes.

  failures are easy to reject, but the distribution-free bound is too loose for

This DVS result is prospective with respect to semantic targets, which were not
executed until the v3 model was frozen, but it is not a final sequestered result:
official test accuracy selected the reference-model pipeline. A fresh external
replication is required before submission.

The feedforward N-MNIST negative control distinguishes a generic statistical
failure from a recurrence-dependent one. Five independently trained models
reach $97.42\pm0.11\%$ reference test accuracy. Reset-to-value loses only 0.29
points on average, one-step delay has effectively zero effect, and the largest
mean loss---floor-rounded fixed point---is 4.27 points. Across its 50 cells the
same simultaneous procedure again has no observed violation, but now its median
slack is 1.15 points and its 90th-percentile slack is 3.03 points, passing both
software tightness gates. Figure~\ref{fig:recurrent-vs-feedforward} summarizes
the contrast. The result supports recurrent amplification as a central part of
the transport problem, but it also falsifies any claim that raw disagreement is
uniformly tight across architectures and semantic regimes.

The static analysis yields the same model-class contrast. On deterministic
128-input subsets from each frozen N-MNIST audit split, the full 16-member family
is soundly certified on $98.59\pm0.65\%$ of inputs, compared with
$38.72\pm3.00\%$ for all 861 SHD audit inputs per seed. Both equal exact finite-
family agreement. Every N-MNIST single-axis family certifies at least 99.5\% on
average, and no static certificate contradicts exact enumeration. This cross-
dataset subset result is diagnostic rather than causal or a primary full-audit
estimate.

A within-SHD intervention isolates the feedback mechanism more directly. We zero
only each trained model's recurrent matrix, retaining its inputs, audit split, and
all other parameters. Full-family static coverage and exact agreement both rise
by 50.59 points to $89.31\pm0.85\%$. No static certificate contradicts exact
enumeration. Because
this intervention changes predictions and margins, it is not an accuracy-matched
architecture comparison. It nevertheless establishes that recurrent feedback is
a major cause of true finite-family semantic instability. It does not explain or
repair the continuous-box abstraction failure. Figure~\ref{fig:recurrence-ablation-static}
summarizes the result.

Across fixed 256-input subsets, mean trained-model finite-family coverage rises
from 30.94\% at ten time bins to 36.17\% at 50 and matches exact agreement to
within 0.08 points throughout. The corresponding zero-recurrence trajectory rises
from 66.72\% to 88.98\%. The upward trend follows accumulating reference margin;
recurrent semantic instability is already present at the earliest measured
horizon but does not monotonically worsen with depth. Shorter unrolls are not the
missing method for the continuous-box failure. Figure~\ref{fig:shd-horizon}
reports the complete five-seed trajectories.

Across five reset-to-value seeds, certificate-directed repair recovers
$77.2\pm4.5\%$ of lost test accuracy, compared with $74.2\pm3.8\%$ for pure
logit distillation and $40.1\pm11.6\%$ for global threshold scaling. It clears
the 70\% recovery target for every seed. This favorable accuracy result does not
yield a deployable certificate: its mean post-repair upper bound is 15.1 points,
no seed is accepted at a five-point budget, and the bound is 1.85 points looser
than logit-only distillation on average. Figure~\ref{fig:shd-repair} makes the
two criteria explicit.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/shd_repair_comparison.pdf}
  \caption{Five-seed, label-free repair of reset-to-value semantics. The

  untouched audit split. Dashed lines are the preregistered targets.}
  \label{fig:shd-repair}
\end{figure}

Repair can therefore restore aggregate accuracy without restoring decision
identity. The current evidence supports the semantic transport problem, repair
opportunity, and exact prospective certification of finite enumerated execution
families. It does \emph{not} support the intended sound bounded-family claim or a
tight recurrent distribution certificate. The next method must preserve
timestep/threshold dependence through recurrent threshold branches; absent that
advance and physical validation, the honest contribution is a narrower finite-
family or systems/risk-ranking paper rather than the intended NeurIPS claim.
