\section{Preregistered evaluation}

The primary event-native tasks are SHD with an SRNN and DVS Gesture with a
convolutional/recurrent SNN. N-MNIST with a feedforward SNN is a negative control
expected to be more transport-robust. SSC is deferred until all primary gates
pass. Five training seeds share frozen sample IDs and semantics variants.

The canonical training set is hash-partitioned into training, label-free repair
calibration, and unlabeled certificate audit splits. The canonical labeled test
set is sequestered. We vary reset, integration, threshold timing, precision,
rounding/saturation, and one-step delay separately, followed by four preregistered
high-risk combinations.

For every condition we will report actual absolute accuracy change, certificate
upper bound and slack, static certified-input fraction, sample complexity,
emulator--hardware disagreement, decisions at one-, two-, and five-point budgets,
repair recovery, remaining risk, label count, runtime, and hardware runs. Single-
axis experiments support causal attribution and provide counterexamples to
invalid equivalence claims.

The submission gate requires two physical backends, four conditions with more
than five points of uncorrected loss, no observed simultaneous-bound violation,
median slack at most three points, 90th-percentile slack at most eight points,
and at least 70\% label-free recovery across both tasks and backends. The method
must beat global threshold scaling and PTQ at matched cost. If more than 80\% of
inputs lack a nonvacuous static family certificate, or conformance dominates, we
pivot to a systems venue.

\subsection{Software evidence and an early formulation failure}

We completed the frozen software matrix on SHD for five independently trained
128-neuron SRNNs. The reference test accuracy is $71.5\pm3.0\%$ (mean and sample
standard deviation); this is sufficient for a semantic-sensitivity study but not
the reference-model quality we require for the final submission. Five of ten
target conditions lose more than five points on average. Reset-to-value loses
9.9 points, reset plus one-step delay loses 16.1 points, and floor-rounded fixed
point loses 53.6 points. Thus the recurrent transport failure is not a
single-seed artifact.

Across all 50 seed--condition cells, unlabeled audit disagreement strongly ranks
absolute test accuracy change (Pearson $r=0.989$, Spearman $\rho=0.957$). The
Bonferroni-corrected 95\% Clopper--Pearson limits have no observed violation.
They are nevertheless too loose for deployment: median slack is 17.6 points and
90th-percentile slack is 30.1 points, failing the preregistered three- and
eight-point gates by a large margin. Figure~\ref{fig:shd-software-pilot} shows
that the failure is not primarily finite-sample confidence inflation; empirical
disagreement itself substantially exceeds net accuracy change.

The sound per-input analysis exposes an important distinction between discrete
and continuous families. An initial implementation merged output intervals from
mutually exclusive discrete semantics before testing the argmax. That operation
is sound but unnecessarily compares, for example, the reference-class logit from
one reset rule with a competitor from another. It certified only 18.79\% of SHD
inputs. We preserve that result but supersede it with member-wise checking:
continuous uncertainty is merged within each discrete member, the argmax is
proved there, and the family certificate is the conjunction of member results.

Across all 861 audit inputs and five seeds, this correction certifies 76.19\% for
reset, 84.81\% for integration, 83.00\% for timing or delay, and 94.96\% for
nearest fixed point. The finite 16-member high-risk family certifies 38.72\%,
ranging from 34.26\% to 42.39\% by seed. For unquantized point semantics it
matches exact family agreement, as it should. No static certificate contradicts
exact enumeration. The finite-family static gate therefore passes; the earlier
failure was an avoidable cross-member relaxation artifact.

The bounded-family result is different. We jointly vary timestep and neuron
threshold by only $\pm1\%$, $\pm2\%$, or $\pm5\%$ inside every one of the 16
discrete members. The sound interval domain certifies zero SHD inputs at every
radius for every seed. On fixed 64-input validation subsets, all tested box
corners preserve the reference prediction on 36.9\%, 32.5\%, and 24.7\% of
inputs, respectively, with no counterexample to a static certificate. Corner
agreement is diagnostic rather than a proof over the interior, but its separation
from zero sound coverage quantifies the abstraction failure.

A five-seed full-audit radius sweep localizes the failure even further. For the
reference discrete semantics alone, joint timestep/threshold coverage averages
89.64\% at relative radius $10^{-6}$, 50.57\% at $10^{-5}$, and zero at
$10^{-4}$. Timestep and threshold varied separately exhibit the same cliff.
Uniform partitions do not provide a practical escape: an $8\times8$ grid at
$\pm1\%$ certifies zero of 128 inputs, and a $16\times16$ grid certifies zero of
64 after 89 seconds. Covering $\pm1\%$ with sub-box radius $10^{-5}$ requires
roughly $10^6$ continuous cells, or $16$ million abstract members after the
discrete family is included. Figure~\ref{fig:shd-continuous} shows that the
declared stop rule still triggers for the promised continuous bounded-family
claim, not for finite enumeration.

A fixed-center-branch analyzer could preserve timestep/threshold dependence more
accurately than intervals, but only while the reference spike trace remains
valid. We test the necessary endpoint condition on all five SHD audits for the
reference discrete semantics. Across the four joint box corners, center-trace
identity is $99.28\%$ at radius $10^{-6}$, $93.98\%$ at $10^{-5}$,
$54.12\%$ at $10^{-4}$, $0.74\%$ at $10^{-3}$, and zero at $10^{-2}$.
Prediction identity at the same radii is $100\%$, $99.98\%$, $99.14\%$,
$96.24\%$, and $89.36\%$. Corner trace identity is not an interior proof, but
any observed corner change rules out a certificate that assumes the center trace
throughout the box. Thus fixed-branch propagation has useful headroom near
$10^{-4}$ but cannot solve the intended $\pm1\%$ claim; the next abstraction
must retain correlations across branch changes. Figure~\ref{fig:branch-stability}
shows the divergence between internal trajectories and decisions.

We next sample the interior rather than only endpoints. On deterministic
128-input subsets, a nested $17\times17$ grid over the reference member contains
$81.67\pm5.44$ distinct spike traces per input on average; the mean seed-level
90th percentile is 120.56, and no input retains a single trace. Nevertheless,
$80.94\pm2.68\%$ of inputs retain one prediction over all 289 points, and only
$4.35\pm0.72\%$ of grid--center pairs disagree. Sampled trace growth is
sublinear in grid size (log--log slope 0.64), but these counts are lower bounds
on the continuous branch count. The result rejects single-trajectory reasoning
while explaining why a correlated decision abstraction may still succeed.
Figure~\ref{fig:branch-complexity} reports the separation.

True instability also limits the attainable coverage. We combine a $9\times9$
interior grid with every member of the 16-way discrete family, for 1,296 sampled
semantics per input. Full-family prediction identity is
$31.09\pm2.78\%$ on the same deterministic 128-input subsets, with every seed
between 28.12\% and 35.16\%. The reference discrete member alone retains
$82.19\pm2.78\%$. Thus a perfect analyzer has only 11.09 points of mean sampled
headroom above the 20\% gate. The sampled quantity is an upper bound on possible
certificate coverage, not a lower bound: additional interior counterexamples
can only reduce it. Figure~\ref{fig:full-family-grid} shows that pre-integration
timing, reset-to-value, delay, and exponential integration compound rather than
contribute interchangeable perturbations.

The Cartesian family is not the only meaningful deployment contract. On the
same grid and inputs, reset, integration, timing, and delay subfamilies retain
60.31\%, 68.91\%, 69.06\%, and 69.06\% mean prediction identity,
respectively; every primary-axis seed remains above 56.25\%. Reset plus delay
retains 46.72\%, integration plus timing 55.47\%, and the full family 31.09\%.
Continuous variation costs 14.7--16.3 points for the single-axis families. This
supports a targeted bounded-axis fallback with substantial attainable headroom,
but does not turn the grid into a proof. Figure~\ref{fig:subfamily-grid} makes
the hierarchy explicit.

We then test the most direct correlated alternative: soundly retain each
possible spike vector as a separate abstract state, merging only states with
identical current spikes and accumulated logits. This prototype is safely
inconclusive when a state cap is exceeded. On seed 1701, no input completes even
the first required cell in staged full-cover tests. With a 65,536-state cap,
neither merged nor unmerged analysis completes any of 72 representative
cell--input runs spanning $32\times32$, $64\times64$, and $128\times128$
partitions; median abort moves only from timestep 21 to 41 of 50. The finest
partition implies 16,384 cells per input before including the other 15 discrete
members. Figure~\ref{fig:branch-set-feasibility} therefore rejects explicit
branch lists as the missing full-model method. Symbolic guard compression or a
decision-level correlated relaxation is required.

The direct decision-level relaxation is also insufficient. On a constructed
witness it soundly certifies a shared-spike margin that separate logit intervals
cannot, and randomized sampled executions remain enclosed. Yet on staged seed-
1701 reset-family audits it certifies zero inputs at every partition count from
$1\times1$ through $32\times32$, exactly matching ordinary intervals (128
inputs through $4\times4$, 32 through $16\times16$, and 16 at $32\times32$).
Thus independent output bounds are not the dominant loss; useful compression
must preserve correlations through recurrent hidden states and threshold guards.
Zero coverage remains an inconclusive abstraction result, not a model failure.

Adaptive allocation does not change this verdict. We select the first inputs
that are stable on all 81 finite grid points, deliberately avoiding observed
counterexamples while testing tractability. Eighty of the first 128 audit-pool
inputs pass that diagnostic. On the first two, adaptive margin analysis retires
no sub-box through 256 leaves; on the first input, it still covers 0\% after
8,191 analyzed boxes and 4,096 final leaves. Thus the problem is not uniform
partition scheduling: no local leaf is provable at the reached resolution.

Relational affine guards change the local result. Ordinary decision intervals
prove zero cell--inputs at every tested resolution; the affine domain proves
3/2,048 at $4\times4$, 306/8,192 (3.74\%) at $8\times8$, and 1,512/8,192
(18.46\%) at $16\times16$. On the same first grid-stable input, adaptive affine
coverage rises from 3.13\% at 64 leaves to 30.66\% at 256, 61.47\% at 1,024,
85.72\% at 4,096, and 95.61\% at 16,384. No full certificate is issued. This is
the first sound domain with nonzero SHD local coverage, but the remaining
guard-surface residue calls for symbolic constraint cuts rather than still finer
axis-aligned boxes.
Using accumulated uncertain-guard generator scores to choose the split axis
raises matched 4,096-leaf coverage only from 85.72\% to 86.35\% (and is slightly
worse at 256 leaves). We therefore stop axis-policy tuning; the next step must
represent non-axis-aligned guard constraints.

Polygonal guard bands provide the strongest result after explicit float32
roundoff accounting. A fixed 128-cut cap gives 82.26\% certified area at 1,024
leaves and 94.13\% at 4,096. Under the scale-invariant one-cut-per-eight-leaves
rule, coverage reaches 99.7557\% at 4,096 and 99.8331\% after 31,060 polygon
analyses at 16,384 leaves. The latter leaves 13,158 tiny polygons and 0.1669\%
area unresolved. Four times the leaves improve coverage by only 0.077 points,
so we stop leaf escalation and issue no certificate: a joint
timestep--threshold boundary oracle is still required.

The exact threshold oracle then exhausts every binary64 threshold scale in the
same plus/minus 1\% range at 257 fixed timestep slices. All 257 slices preserve
the reference class, using 2,716 exact trace cells in 12.1 seconds and at most
17 cells for any slice. This removes threshold-only relaxation as the cause at
those slices, but the open timestep regions between slices remain unproved; the
result is therefore diagnostic, not a joint continuous-family certificate.

Exporting the 4,096-leaf residual makes the gap concrete. Its 3,365 polygons
have median timestep-factor width $4.19\times10^{-5}$, below the
$7.81\times10^{-5}$ exact-slice spacing; 1,271 polygons lie entirely between
the 257 slices. Their projections collectively span both full parameter axes.
Thus denser uniform slicing does not compose into a proof: the next oracle must
consume the residual polygon constraints directly.

A targeted falsification search evaluates every residual vertex, edge midpoint,
and centroid plus 32 deterministic random convex points per polygon. All 128,460
unique executions preserve class 2; the smallest reference-class margin is
1.033. This supports abstraction slack rather than a hidden sampled flip, but
does not certify any unsampled point.

The five frozen convolutional/recurrent DVS Gesture models reach
$84.85\pm1.76\%$ reference test accuracy. Floor-rounded fixed point loses 36.6
points on average, whereas exponential integration loses 3.7 points and
reset-to-value loses 1.5 points; only the two floor conditions exceed five
points on average. Disagreement again ranks absolute test change (Pearson
$r=0.962$), and none of 50 simultaneous limits is violated. The certificate is
still operationally loose: median slack is 18.5 points and 90th-percentile slack
is 29.4 points. With only 104 audit recordings per seed, simultaneous confidence
also imposes a roughly five-point floor near zero observed disagreement.
Figure~\ref{fig:dvs-software} shows both regimes.

This DVS result is prospective with respect to semantic targets, which were not
executed until the v3 model was frozen, but it is not a final sequestered result:
official test accuracy selected the reference-model pipeline. A fresh external
replication is required before submission.

We nevertheless use this frozen development matrix to test cross-architecture
repair on its severe floor-rounded condition. Certificate-directed and
logit-only methods expose the same 960 per-channel scale and bias parameters,
use all 97 calibration recordings without labels, and receive 280 updates.
Source-initialized QAT uses all 97 labels, exposes 678,560 parameters, and
receives the same updates. Every selected checkpoint is then evaluated on the
disjoint 104-recording audit split with a ten-way corrected
$\alpha=0.005$ Clopper--Pearson limit.

Certificate-directed repair recovers $91.1\pm7.8\%$ of the 36.6-point mean
fixed-point loss and clears 70\% for every seed. Logit-only recovers
$89.3\pm8.4\%$, global threshold scaling $74.8\pm8.6\%$, and labeled QAT
$99.6\pm10.8\%$. The proposed objective beats logit-only in only three of five
paired seeds; one seed ties global scaling. Mean remaining accuracy loss is 3.11
points for the proposed repair and 0.76 points for QAT. Yet their mean bounds
are nearly identical and loose---21.49 and 22.31 points---while logit-only is
tighter at 15.11 points. No repaired seed certifies a five-point budget. Thus
the cross-task result supports restricted label-free repair, but not objective
superiority or certificate restoration. Figure~\ref{fig:dvs-repair} summarizes
the separation. It inherits the development-only limitation above.

The feedforward N-MNIST negative control distinguishes a generic statistical
failure from a recurrence-dependent one. Five independently trained models
reach $97.42\pm0.11\%$ reference test accuracy. Reset-to-value loses only 0.29
points on average, one-step delay has effectively zero effect, and the largest
mean loss---floor-rounded fixed point---is 4.27 points. Across its 50 cells the
same simultaneous procedure again has no observed violation, but now its median
slack is 1.15 points and its 90th-percentile slack is 3.03 points, passing both
software tightness gates. Figure~\ref{fig:recurrent-vs-feedforward} summarizes
the contrast. The result supports recurrent amplification as a central part of
the transport problem, but it also falsifies any claim that raw disagreement is
uniformly tight across architectures and semantic regimes.

The static analysis yields the same model-class contrast. On deterministic
128-input subsets from each frozen N-MNIST audit split, the full 16-member family
is soundly certified on $98.59\pm0.65\%$ of inputs, compared with
$38.72\pm3.00\%$ for all 861 SHD audit inputs per seed. Both equal exact finite-
family agreement. Every N-MNIST single-axis family certifies at least 99.5\% on
average, and no static certificate contradicts exact enumeration. This cross-
dataset subset result is diagnostic rather than causal or a primary full-audit
estimate.

A within-SHD intervention isolates the feedback mechanism more directly. We zero
only each trained model's recurrent matrix, retaining its inputs, audit split, and
all other parameters. Full-family static coverage and exact agreement both rise
by 50.59 points to $89.31\pm0.85\%$. No static certificate contradicts exact
enumeration. Because
this intervention changes predictions and margins, it is not an accuracy-matched
architecture comparison. It nevertheless establishes that recurrent feedback is
a major cause of true finite-family semantic instability. It does not explain or
repair the continuous-box abstraction failure. Figure~\ref{fig:recurrence-ablation-static}
summarizes the result.

Table~\ref{tab:sample-complexity} isolates confidence inflation at zero observed
disagreement. Under the frozen ten-way Bonferroni correction, one-, two-, and
five-point budgets require at least 528, 263, and 104 paired audit samples,
respectively. DVS Gesture therefore cannot accept one- or two-point budgets even
in the best observed case. SHD and N-MNIST exceed all three zero-error sample
requirements; SHD looseness is consequently driven by empirical disagreement,
not audit size.

The looseness is also not removable by a different confidence interval alone.
For every one of the 150 stored audit prediction pairs (three benchmarks, five
seeds, and ten targets), we construct two label vectors without reading dataset
labels: one equals the reference predictions and one equals the target
predictions. In all 150 cells, the resulting signed accuracy changes attain the
empirical disagreement rate in both directions exactly, as predicted by the
sharpness proposition. Thus the observed gap between disagreement and actual
accuracy change cannot support a claim that a uniformly tighter label-free
absolute-accuracy certificate exists. Such a claim must state and validate new
assumptions or use labels.

We next compare repair on reset-to-value and floor-rounded Q8-weight/Q16-state
execution. Every gradient method uses the same 800 calibration inputs, 40
epochs, 280 optimizer updates, and GPU. Certificate-directed and logit-only
calibration use no labels and expose 640 parameters. The source-initialized
labeled target fine-tune---the QAT baseline when fixed point is active---and
random-initialized target retraining use all 800 labels and expose 108,928
parameters. Model selection uses calibration behavior only; the 861-input audit
and labeled test sets remain untouched until the selected model is frozen.

On reset-to-value, certificate-directed repair recovers $77.2\pm4.5\%$ of lost
test accuracy, compared with $74.2\pm3.8\%$ for logit-only calibration,
$40.1\pm11.6\%$ for global threshold scaling, and $73.5\pm17.8\%$ for the
labeled source-initialized fine-tune. The proposed method clears 70\% recovery
for every seed and leaves a mean 2.23-point accuracy loss. Yet its mean
post-repair upper bound is 15.1 points, 1.85 points looser than logit-only, and
no seed is accepted at a five-point budget.

Floor rounding is the genuine QAT comparison and begins with a 53.62-point mean
loss. Certificate-directed repair recovers $87.8\pm5.1\%$ and beats unrepaired
PTQ and global threshold scaling in every seed. However, logit-only recovers
$90.1\pm2.8\%$, while 800-label QAT recovers $101.5\pm3.4\%$ and slightly
exceeds reference accuracy. The proposed method leaves a 6.48-point mean loss
and a 19.1-point mean certificate; QAT's certificate is still 16.0 points.
Matched-budget from-scratch retraining is much weaker: it recovers 58.4\% on
floor rounding and increases the reset loss. This is a constrained baseline,
not an estimate of retraining with the full labeled training set.

Across all ten certificate-directed seed/condition cells, recovery exceeds 70\%
and beats both no repair and global scaling, but the proposed objective is not
uniformly better than logit-only. None of the 50 method/seed/condition cells
certifies a five-point budget; no observed accuracy change violates its 95\%
bound. Figure~\ref{fig:shd-repair} separates accuracy restoration from restored
decision identity.

We additionally ask whether repair increases the attainable ceiling over a
local target family. Taking the original source/reference predictions as the
contract, we execute each selected reset-target model over the $9\times9$
$\pm1\%$ grid. No repair retains 64.84\% identity. Certificate-directed repair
raises this to $75.00\pm3.43\%$ and improves all five seeds; logit-only reaches
$76.41\pm3.53\%$, global scaling 68.91\%, and the labeled target fine-tune
71.09\%. Thus repair creates roughly ten points of extra sampled certification
headroom, but the proposed objective remains 1.41 points below logit-only.

The sound-analysis outcome is less favorable. On seed 1701 representative
reset-family cells with a 65,536-state cap, none of the no-repair,
certificate-directed, or logit-only models completes. At $128\times128$
partitioning, the mean cell-median abort timestep is 47.0 without repair, 42.83
after certificate-directed repair, and 40.33 after logit-only repair. Hence the
current proxy restores sampled decisions while making explicit branch proof no
easier. We do not claim certificate restoration; the next repair objective must
directly enlarge verified guard margins or reduce proof complexity.

A calibration-only, single-seed guard-margin ablation tests that prescription
without entering the primary matrix. With the selected weight, 40 epochs yield
79.6\% accuracy recovery, 73.44\% sampled grid-family identity, and mean abort
timestep 44.42 at $128\times128$ partitioning. This partially improves on the
42.83 reached by certificate-directed repair, but remains below 47.0 without
repair and completes no cell. Stronger guard weights reduce accuracy recovery

We next optimize the worst per-input reference-class loss over either the four
family corners or a $3\times3$ family grid. The best single-seed development
checkpoint is the 10-epoch $3\times3$ model: its denser $9\times9$ audit-grid
identity is 75.00\%, one input above certificate-directed repair's 74.22\% but
below logit-only's 77.34\%. Extending the identical objective to 40 epochs
reduces calibration family disagreements from 131 to 106, yet audit-grid
identity falls to 71.88\%. We stop before sound branch analysis and five seeds:
sampled-family loss does not reliably predict untouched family identity, much
less a continuous-family certificate.

Repair can therefore restore aggregate accuracy without restoring decision
identity. The current evidence supports the semantic transport problem, a
substantial label-free repair opportunity, and exact prospective certification
of finite enumerated execution families. It does \emph{not} establish a unique
advantage for the certificate-directed objective over simpler logit imitation,
nor support the intended sound bounded-family claim or a tight recurrent
distribution certificate. The next method must preserve timestep/threshold
dependence through recurrent threshold branches; absent that advance and
physical validation, the honest contribution is a narrower finite-family or
systems/risk-ranking paper rather than the intended NeurIPS claim.
